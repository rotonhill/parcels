322 FLAX HILL RD

Year Built: 1900 | Parcel: 5-77-137-0

This was the home of John Fodor (~1866-1932). Fodor was a Hungarian immigrant who came to this country in 1886, and owned this property by at least 1900. His wife and children lived here almost all of the 1900s. In the 1990s, their farm became a municipal open space.

328 FLAX HILL RD

Year Built: 1903 | Parcel: 5-77-138-0

Although the parcel data says this house was built in 1903, the history of this house goes back to about 1810, when it was constructed by Eliphalet Lockwood.

A city history says that Lockwood sold the place to Nehemiah Hanford, who also appears on a historical marker down the road at 347 Flax Hill Rd.

In the 1930s this was the address of the Norwalk Rug Cleaners, a venture of Joseph Fodor.

The building is now the home of the Norwalk Preservation Trust, among other related organizations.

Fodor Farm

Year Built: 0 | Parcel: 5-77-5-0

This area was for hundreds of years a working farm, with orchards and animals and small-scale horticulture. In that way, it was exactly like all the surrounding area. What sets this particular plot apart is that it was the last remaining section in this neighborhood that hadn't already been subdivided and developed.

So when the Fodor family sold the land in the 1990s with the intention of putting up 50+ homes, just like everyone else had been doing for decades, there was a grassroots effort to preserve the property as open space. Eventually the city acquired the land and created a durable open space for the community.

341 FLAX HILL RD

Year Built: 1780 | Parcel: 5-73B-22-0

This was the homestead of Sally Ann Keeler, who inherited much of the land around here when her father David Lockwood died in 1835. The original house was described as a 12-room, two story house, with an attic. Of course that means 12 rooms in total, not just bedrooms. That house burned to the ground in 1912, and was replaced that year by William G Shaw. There's much more detail about all this in The Keeler Property.

347 FLAX HILL RD

Year Built: 1760 | Parcel: 5-73B-20-0

Captain Ira Ford lived here from the 1840s until his death in 1858. His wife was the former Phebe Bouton, whose sister Hannah lived nearby at 354 Highland Ave.

This house has a Norwalk Historical Society plaque that notes that George and Mary Platt lived here in the 1860s.

Alexander and Maria Rummler lived here in the early 1900s. Alexander was an artist and sat on the Board of Education. Maria was the first woman elected to the Norwalk City Council.

There's a second historical plaque on this property, on another building behind the main house, that reads "Nehemiah Hanford House c1780".

378 FLAX HILL RD

Year Built: 1792 | Parcel: 5-78-5-0

In 1865 this was the home of Josiah Tristram (1827-1886), who played a role in the Adams Express Train Robbery. The first arrests took place here under the watchful eye of famous detective Alan Pinkerton.

Josiah's son William Henry Tristram (1865-1931) was the popular postman on the Rowayton rural route. He lived here until the early 1920s.

384 FLAX HILL RD

Year Built: 1865 | Parcel: 5-78-4-0

In the 1930s and 1940s, this was the home of Calvin Ira Crocker (1875-1951). Crocker was the Chief Engineer for the New York ferries. He was also the Chief Engineer for the construction of the Queenboro Bridge, and in 1927 was described as the Chief Engineer for the Brooklyn and Manhattan Bridges.

Crocker's wife was the former Mary Lavinia Broadhurst (1885-1968), and this was her house. She had inherited the house from her parents in the 1920s, and in fact she lived there for many years after Calvin died.

388 FLAX HILL RD

Year Built: 1774 | Parcel: 5-78-3-0

This house has a Norwalk Historical Society plaque that reads "Lewis Raymond Sr House c. 1810". This house is wood framed, but Lewis Raymond Jr was a brickmason, so his nearby house at 26 Keeler is made of brick.

In the 1910s this was the home of artist and architect Harry Stacey Benton (1877-1947) and his then-wife Elsie N. Benton (1878-1953). Elsie ran a Norwalk interior design firm called Yonder Studio. They later moved around the corner to 377 Highland, but they also owned much of the land behind these houses, land that is today Topping Lane. Their son Burt Benton lived at 5 Topping Lane.

In the 1930s this was the home of Douglas Pairman Hall, a well-known architect specializing in theaters, and his wife Aimee Green Hall.

Year Built: 1924 | Parcel: 5-78-2-0

459 FLAX HILL RD

Year Built: 1862 | Parcel: 5-72-3-0

This was the old location of Brookside School, one of the oldest schools in Norwalk. It is unclear when it opened, but in 1901 Samuel Richards Reed spoke about attending the school back in 1847, so it was open by then, at least. The school was shut down in 1939, and its replacement opened on Highland Ave. in 1954.

This property was later purchased by Harry J. Getner, the president of the Connecticut Ice Machine Company, who had attended the school as a child. In the 1960s he remodeled the building, keeping its character but giving it a new life as a residence.

Traendly & Schenck Greenhouses

Year Built: 1912 | Parcel: TraendlyGreenhouses

Traendly & Schenck were the largest supplier of flowers to New York City, all grown in 50 greenhouses on either side of Five Mile River in Norwalk and Darien. Traendly operated from about 1912 or so until the late 1950s. When they shut down the greenhouses they tried to sell this land to Sperry-Rand for a manufacturing facility, but their bruising 3-year attempt to re-zone the area was ultimately overturned due to fierce opposition from the local community.

The Rockledge Mansion at 40 Highland Ave

Year Built: 1913 | Parcel: 6-16C-1-0

James Augustine Farrell, the President of U.S. Steel, first built a mansion on this property in 1913. That mansion immediately burned down, so in 1916 he built a second one, and that one's still standing (see the whole story at Historic Rowayton).

After the Farrells died in the early 1940s, Darien resident James Rand, the Chairman of Remington-Rand, bought the mansion as a headquarters for his company. The first practical commercial computer, the UNIVAC I, was developed in the barns across Highland Ave, now the Rowayton Library. Post-war Remington-Rand executives Douglas MacArthur and Leslie R. Groves worked out of this location.

297 HIGHLAND AVE

Year Built: 1858 | Parcel: 5-80-30-0

This house has a Norwalk Historical Society plaque that reads "Ira Ford Hoyt House, c. 1859". It was built by Ira Ford Hoyt (1824-1887), who was a lumber dealer and builder. His mother Hannah Smith Hoyt lived up the street in 354 Highland Ave, and his namesake Captain Ira Ford lived in 347 Flax Hill Rd.

Ira's son Dudley Ely Hoyt (1866-1921) eventually took over the house. He was also a builder, who among other things built the original Norwalk Yacht Club building. Dudley must have named after Dudley Pettibone Ely, the first mayor of South Norwalk.

Dudley had a daughter named Margaret Rachel Hoyt (1905-2001), later known as Margaret Hoyt Smith. Margaret became the first female architect in Norwalk. She designed a part of the cap of the Chrysler building, the Norwalk Historical Society Museum, and a number of houses in Rowayton. Margaret took over the family home from her parents and raised her children there. In 1959 she sold the land to the city so it could build Brien McMahon High School. The 100-year-old house was moved across the street to its present location.

354 HIGHLAND AVE

Year Built: 1795 | Parcel: 5-81-1-0

This was the home Aaron Gregory Hoyt and his wife Hannah Smith Bouton. Aaron died in 1836, and Hannah lived here until her death in 1873. Their son Ira Ford Hoyt, who would eventually live down the street at 297 Highland Ave, was named after their brother-in-law Captain Ira Ford. Hannah's sister Phebe went to Troy to marry the steamboat Captain, but when he retired they moved back to Norwalk in 347 Flax Hill Rd, just up the street.

Aaron and Hannah are buried in the tiny Hoyt-Bouton Cemetery on Witch Lane.

3 ROCHELLE RD

Year Built: 1935 | Parcel: 5-73B-21-0

The home currently at 3 Rochelle originally faced Flax Hill. The lot that is now 343 Flax Hill was its front yard, with a large circular driveway. When the lot was split in 1979 the house was reversed to face Rochelle.

From about 1936 until his death in 1954, this was the home of Claude E. Hooper, who measured radio audience sizes, much like Nielsen later did for television. In the late 1940s radio people would ask each other "How's your Hooper?" to see how popular their shows were.

Original Reynolds Farms and Distillery

Year Built: 1922 | Parcel: OriginalReynoldsFarm

This was approximately the original site of the Reynolds Farms Nursery. The Reynolds family also operated a cider mill and distillery here. After the repeal of the 18th Amendment, Reynolds was the first legal distillery in Norwalk, producing 600 cases weekly.

When I-95 came through in the late 1950s Stephen Reynolds retired and the business was purchased by a former employee who moved it to the present location on Richards Ave.

15 WESTVIEW LN

Year Built: 1927 | Parcel: 5-73B-10-0

This was the first home built by Godfrey Shaw on Westview Lane. Shaw would go on to build most of the other houses on the lane over the following decade. In about 1975 the large original lot was subdivided into seven smaller parcels.

The Shaws apparently lived in the house initially, and then in 1931 sold the place to Helen and Howard Rankin Wilkes (1905-1996). Howard Wilkes was a stock broker, and led the effort to save the old Brookside School on Flax Hill Road. When that failed, he led the parent's association in advocating for the construction of the current school on Highland Ave.

In 1946 this house was purchased by Marcell and Jane Rand. Marcell was the son of James Rand, the Chairman of Remington-Rand (later Sperry-Rand), and was the Vice President of International Operations.
